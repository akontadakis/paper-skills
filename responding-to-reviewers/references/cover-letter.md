# Cover Letter to the Editor

**Load when:** writing the cover letter to the editor, in any round.

**Contents:** Goal · Who Reads It · Structure — Revision Cover Letter · Example · Rules · Second-Round Cover Letter · Reject-and-Resubmit Cover Letter · Checklist

## Goal

Give the editor what they need to route the revision, in under a page.

This file covers **revision and resubmission** letters only. The cover letter for an
*initial* submission — fit, contribution, originality statements, suggested reviewers — is
in `research-paper-writing/references/submission-package.md`.

## Who Reads It

The handling editor, for about ninety seconds. They want three things: was every point
addressed, what changed substantively, and is anything left for them to arbitrate.

They will not read the point-by-point unless something here sends them there.

## Structure — Revision Cover Letter

Six short blocks. Write it last, after the response letter is finished.

1. **Address and identifiers** — editor name if known, manuscript title and ID, journal,
   decision date.
2. **The ask** — one sentence: you are resubmitting a revised manuscript in response to the
   decision of `[insert the decision date]`.
3. **Confirmation of completeness** — one sentence: every comment has been addressed
   point by point in the accompanying response.
4. **Summary of substantive changes** — 3–6 numbered lines. Only changes that affect the
   science. Not typographical corrections.
5. **Points requiring the editor's attention** — contested points, reviewer conflicts,
   anything you declined. One line each, or omit the block if there are none.
6. **Close** — one sentence. No flourish.

## Example

The examples in this guide are invented.

> Dear Professor [insert the editor's name],
>
> We are resubmitting manuscript [insert the ID], "[insert the title]", revised in response to
> the reviewers' comments of [insert the date]. Every comment is answered, point by point, in the accompanying response
> document. Four requests were not carried out in full; they are listed below.
>
> The substantive changes are:
>
> 1. A grid convergence test has been added (new §2.4, Table 3). All results have been rerun
>    at 0.5 m spacing. The direction of the headline finding is unchanged; under the
>    worst-case view direction the mean compliant plan depth changed from 2.9 m to 3.1 m
>    (6.9%), as the response to comment R1.1 states.
> 2. The main claim has been bounded to the four sampled climates, in the Abstract, §4.1, and
>    the Conclusion.
> 3. A sensitivity analysis has been added as §4.3 (comment R1.5).
>
> Five points may need your attention:
>
> - Reviewer 1 asks for aggregation by orientation (comment R1.4) and Reviewer 2 for
>   disaggregation by hour (comment R2.5).
>   Both cannot sit in the main text within the length limit. We have kept the orientation
>   aggregation and moved the hourly data to Appendix B, and would welcome your view.
> - We have not added the comparison with Method X requested by Reviewer 2. It requires
>   bidirectional transmission data that does not exist for these assemblies; our reasoning is
>   in the response to comment R2.3 and now in §2.5.
> - We have not added the two further climates requested by Reviewer 1 (comment R1.5). A
>   sensitivity analysis is reported in §4.3 in their place, and the response gives our reason.
> - We have not reported the solver wall-clock time requested by Reviewer 2 (comment R2.8).
>   It was not recorded for the runs behind the results, and recovering it would mean
>   repeating them.
> - We have not restructured the Discussion as Reviewer 3 suggests (comment R3.1). The
>   restructuring would undo the scope bounding that Reviewer 1 required in comment R1.2.
>
> We are grateful to the reviewers; the paper is stronger for their comments.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> [insert the authors]

## Rules

1. **One page maximum.** Half a page is better.
2. **Never duplicate the point-by-point.** Summarise, and point there.
3. **Never hide a refusal.** An editor who discovers a declined request inside the
   point-by-point, unflagged, will read it as concealment.
4. **Flag conflicts explicitly.** Arbitrating between reviewers is the editor's job, and they
   can only do it if you name the conflict.
5. **No new arguments.** Anything worth arguing goes in the point-by-point.
6. **No numbers that are not in the manuscript.**
7. Keep the tone flat. Gratitude in one sentence, at the end.

## Second-Round Cover Letter

Shorter still.

1. Confirm the remaining points are addressed.
2. List only what changed since the previous revision.
3. If a reviewer repeated a point you contested, say how you have handled it this time — the
   editor is watching that specific thread.

> Dear Professor [insert the editor's name],
>
> We are resubmitting [insert the ID] following the second round of review. Reviewer 2's
> remaining concern, [insert the concern], is addressed by [insert what was changed, and
> where]. Reviewer 1's comments [insert their
> nature] are answered in [insert the locations]. [insert any other change made since the
> previous revision, or "No other changes have been made" once `changes.yml` confirms it].

## Reject-and-Resubmit Cover Letter

Treated as a new submission, so the letter does more work.

1. State that this is a resubmission of [insert the ID], with the original decision date.
2. Say what fundamentally changed — not the list of fixes, the change in the work itself.
3. Then the standard summary of changes.
4. If new authors were added for new work, explain the contribution per CRediT roles. Every
   author, old and new, must agree to the change, and the journal's instructions say whether
   the editor must approve it. Check them before the letter says it.

The editor's question here is whether this is genuinely a different paper. Answer it in the
first two sentences.

## Checklist

1. Under one page?
2. Does it confirm every comment was addressed?
3. Are the substantive changes listed, and only those?
4. Is every contested or declined point flagged, with one line of reason?
5. Are reviewer conflicts named, with your choice and a request for guidance?
6. Does it avoid repeating the point-by-point?
7. Does it introduce any number or claim not in the manuscript?
8. Is the manuscript ID, title, and decision date correct?
